organum

manifestum organi (widget): status classificatus per campum (vita, rhythmus, sedes, aspectus, referentia), ligamina, portae, actiones; canon insulae EX CAMPIS GENERATUR (definitio una).

The widget manifest: one STML file per widget (organum is the house word for one) that classifies each field of its state, and from which the rest is generated. A field declares its type, its lifetime (persistent across sessions, transient, or test-only), its rhythm (discrete, or in motion and written only in quiet), where it lives (the island or motus), its one writer, an aspect such as popover, and a reference into a bound document with what to do when the target vanishes; the manifest also lists the widget's bindings, ports and actions. From the fields the loader generates the island's canon, the owner table and the starting values: one definition, where each widget had kept canon files and an owner table by hand. Judgment is in two layers on purpose. The manifest's own canon checks shape and citations; the loader checks the rules that a canon's doctrine refuses to hold, co-occurrence and split values (a field in motus must be in motion and transient; a policy for orphans needs a reference). The loader never reads a file: the caller hands it the text and adds the common base, and organum_montatio is the caller that every mount shares. Written 2026-10-10 (status plan S1 to S4) out of a census, begun while designing a calendar, which found that live widgets classified their state inconsistently. Fran's rule for it was hand-written before generated: no generator and no lints until three widgets have been migrated by hand. pictor was the first widget loaded from its manifest (22 fields of its own) and refuses to mount without it; scriba (16) and the root of vicus (13) followed on 10-12, and their hand-written canon files are gone. The systems conservatio, relatio and dimissio act on what it declares.
