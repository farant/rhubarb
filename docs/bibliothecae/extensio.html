Corpora numerorum algebraicorum EXACTA: Q(alpha)

Exact algebraic number fields, Q(alpha) = Q[t]/f with f monic and irreducible over Z. An Algebraicus is a numerator polynomial in alpha over a positive integer denominator, in a unique canonical form, and it carries its field: mixing two fields gives an invalid element that propagates like NaN, and fields are compared by pointer. Written 2026-10-08 because knotapel's quaternion demos live in Q(sqrt 2), Q(sqrt 3) and Q(sqrt 5) and had done that arithmetic in doubles, deduplicating by tolerance. Named families: extensio_quadratica and extensio_cosinus, Q(cos 2 pi/n); extensio_ex_polynomio takes any f, checking what can be checked (monic, square-free, no rational root) and leaving irreducibility above degree 3 to the caller's word. A field may carry a chosen real root, picked by index from the smallest, and then algebraicus_signum is exact: it bisects the root's interval until zero is excluded, and a zero certificate from the norm (a non-zero value cannot be smaller than 1/M^(d-1)) turns a reducible f into a refusal and not an endless loop. algebraicus_ad_ostendendum prints decimals in which every digit is certified. There is no general compositum, which would need factoring over Q: every real abelian field lies in some Q(cos 2 pi/n) (Kronecker-Weber), so a square root arrives by a Gauss sum, verified before it is returned, and elements are embedded into one cosine field. The reviews found no wrong arithmetic but a series of cost problems, and a planted fault that ate 80 GB led to every loop being bounded by construction. extensio_anulus hands matrix a field.
