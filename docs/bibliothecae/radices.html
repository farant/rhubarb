Radices reales polynomiorum integrorum, EXACTE

Real roots of integer polynomials, exactly. A RadixRealis is a square-free primitive polynomial together with an interval that holds exactly one of its roots, or a single point when the root is found to be rational; radices_reales returns all distinct real roots in ascending order. Isolation is Descartes' rule of signs with bisection (the Vincent-Collins-Akritas method) starting from the Cauchy bound, and a root that falls exactly on a midpoint is returned as that rational. Its reason to exist is comparison across different polynomials: extensio already isolates roots for its own ordered fields but refuses to mix fields, while radix_compara decides the sign of a - b for any two roots, settling equality by a greatest common divisor and never by interval width. radix_signum_polynomii gives the sign of another polynomial at a root, radix_angusta narrows an interval, and radix_ad_chordam prints decimals truncated toward zero in which every digit is certain. Written 2026-10-09. The first review found one wrong answer: an exact point had been taken to mean a linear polynomial, so sqrt 2 compared equal to the rational root 1 of (t - 1)(t^2 - 2); an exact point says only that the root is rational. The first consumer (knotapel demo 123, a polynomial of degree 89) then exposed the cost, and three changes followed: signs at rational points by an integer Horner scheme with no gcds, an in-place Taylor shift in polynomium, and private scratch arenas so that only results reach the caller's arena. That took the demo from 4.8 s and 3.1 GB to 1.4 s and 33 MB. Every limit refuses and never guesses. Oracle: Python decimals at 120 digits. Consumed by knotapel demos 123 and 124.
