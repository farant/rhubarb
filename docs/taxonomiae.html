Taxonomiae

The taxonomies the book sorts things into

A taxonomy here is only useful if it is not nominal: every tier must be told apart by a real trait it shares, a test you can apply to an item, and not by how its name sounds. An item that passes no tier's test is a signal: either it is two things fused, or the tiers are wrong.

Lex

Adopted 2026-10-02. Rare.

A law is held, in our humble judgement (Fran's), with more confidence than a theory warrants, though it is not generally accepted and we may never convince anyone else of it. It is not vision: vision is what we intend, and a law is how things are, whatever we intend. The vision stands on the laws.

Lex holds of the world, not of a project; held beyond the confidence of theory; Fran's judgement alone puts it here; very few always the instances are listed on Officia

Load-bearing decision: where prior art is read

Load-bearing decisions are where we look for prior art, in two directions:

  • Novel properties to adopt. Prior art that did something no one else did, worth taking: Fossil keeping a whole repository in one SQLite file; Unison storing definitions by content hash.
  • Mistakes to learn from. The inverse: where most of the prior art went wrong, the outlier is the one that didn't make the mistake. Make trusted modification times, and Bazel, Nix and ccache each moved to content hashes; darcs and pijul made semantic patches load-bearing, and the patch algebra ate the project; v1 of Lapide anchored its index to character offsets, and every edit broke it.

So the precedents a source cites (tagged "fits no tier: precedent") mostly belong to its load-bearing decisions: they are the evidence a decision was made against.

Principium: the problem side

The tiers have two sides. The telos is the end. A principium is a condition: what is so, that other things are a function of. Regula, project vision and load-bearing decision are interventions: what we keep, build and choose in answer to it. A statement that carries both ("anchor rot is the disease, identity is the cure") is two items fused, and should be split: the condition is a principium, the cure is an intervention.

How a principium is tested. Mostly not by new evidence, but by intervening. Build something that addresses it. Either what depends on it resolves, or the attempt reveals a deeper condition: "I wish I had something that let me manage my files this way"; it gets built; it does not fix your relationship to files; it shows the problem underneath. The first principium is then not refuted but superseded by the deeper one, and the lineage is worth keeping. Example from the day this was written: "document each library" produced markdown files that did not resolve anything, and showed a deeper condition: no one can read 47 hours of per-library prose, and the reader needs the vision, not the functions.

History: principium and regula were one tier ("principle") until 2026-10-02, when Fran split them (a condition is true or false; a commitment is kept or broken), then sharpened principium the same day: "prime condition" rather than "load-bearing", no solution inside it, and tested by intervention. An observation that is general but is not a prime condition is a reason, not a principium. Precedents (from recall, unchecked): Aristotle, Posterior Analytics II.19, principles reached by induction; Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I-II q.94 a.2, speculative and practical first principles; Rittel and Webber (1973), wicked problems are understood only through attempted solutions; Schön, The Reflective Practitioner (1983), the situation "talks back".

Occasio: the opening

Added 2026-10-02 (Fran). An occasio (opportunity) is also on the condition side, but it is not a principium. The test that separates them: what happens if you ignore it? Ignore a principium and things fail. Ignore an occasio and you only miss a gain.

Occasio
opportunity		 situational: it exists now and may close; it points at a specific intervention it makes cheap or possible; ignoring it costs a gain, not a failure a window FreeType and HarfBuzz already parse the same tables, so one parser serves both; the conformance corpora already exist; AI changes what one person can carry

Conditions that enable rather than constrain ("the oracles already exist", "PDF is the display list") were first filed as principia; they are occasiones. Occasio, like the Greek Kairos, was pictured with a forelock and a bald back of the head: seize it as it comes (from recall).

Indicium: the clue

Added 2026-10-02 (Fran). An indicium (clue) is a trend or a sign that is suggestive: it does not establish anything, it points in a direction. Others moving the same way is the typical clue.

Indicium
clue		 suggestive, not conclusive; directional: it points at a hypothesis; dated: it is an event or a trend, and trends reverse; if ignored, nothing fails now, but you may be late or wrong while the trend holds the HarfBuzz maintainers moved HarfBuzz onto a shared font parser; Chrome dropped FreeType; ICU's own consortium rebuilt it as modules

Not a principium: enough independent clues can be distilled into one, so clues are its raw material. Not a precedent: a precedent is read for what it did (a property to adopt, a mistake to avoid), a clue for where things are going. Not an occasio: an occasio is an opening to take, a clue a sign to read. Prior art (from recall): Ginzburg's "evidential paradigm" (paradigma indiziario, 1979), reasoning from small traces as Morelli, Holmes and Freud did; Peirce's abduction, from a clue to a hypothesis.

Not to be confused with a design pattern: a regula binds everywhere, kept or broken; a pattern describes a context, a recurring solution and when it does not fit, and is used where its context holds (Fran, 2026-10-02: rules risk being followed "in every circumstance").

The traits behind the tiers

  • What it serves: asking "why?" of an item should land exactly one tier up.
  • Horizon: how long until it is realised, or how long it stays true.
  • Rate of revision: how often it would change.
  • Scope: all projects, or one.
  • Cost of reversal: what breaks if it changes (the "load-bearing walls vs furniture" line in tabularium-visio).
  • Side: condition (problem or opening) or intervention (solution).
  • If ignored: things fail (principium) or a gain is missed (occasio).

Open idea: record these traits for each item and derive the tier from them, so that nothing is filed as a principle because it sounds grand.

Prior art

From recall, not yet checked against the sources: Aristotle's ordering of ends (Nicomachean Ethics I.1–2); Rasmussen's abstraction hierarchy (up answers why, down answers how); Jaques' time span of discretion (Requisite Organization); Brand's pace layering (The Clock of the Long Now); Cockburn's use-case goal levels (the coffee-break test). The nominal counter-example: business vision / mission / strategy statements.
