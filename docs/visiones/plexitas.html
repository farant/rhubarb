II. The thesis

  • X6Higher-dimensional shapes are best explained as generated by 3D threads; a knotapel is a program whose execution yields those shapes L54 Project vision
  • X7The project is engineering a representation that fits well, not proving that one exists L54, L252 Principium
  • X8Universality is free: any reasonable compact shape is generated from threads (Hahn–Mazurkiewicz), every compact smooth manifold is the configuration space of a simple mechanism (Kapovich–Millson) L56 for X7Fits no tier: reason
  • X9What is open: whether knotapels are an *efficient* representation (compact, local, natural) against Hilbert, Fourier and wavelet baselines L57 Fits no tier: open question
  • X10A knotapel is a *framed geometric knot*: a specific conformation whose geometry, not just topology, carries information L58, L36 Load-bearing decision
  • X11The interpreter is a pure function (knotapel, positions of k heads, optional refinement level) → n coordinates; one head yields a curve, k heads a k-dimensional shape L59, L244 Load-bearing decision
  • X12The pipeline: hypergraph source → textile-style physical relaxation (compiler) → geometric knot (executable) → interpreter → intrinsic n-manifold (output) L60, L225 Project vision
  • X13The claim of good fit "lives entirely in" the constant of Kolmogorov invariance between universal languages L252 for X7Fits no tier: reason

IV. Coordinates

  • X20Thread coordinates (z, θ) per thread; N threads give 2N dimensions; crossings lock the coordinate systems and between crossings they vary as a gradient L95-L97 Fits no tier: reason
  • X21Frames: position plus frame gives 3 parameters; the frame is SO(3), double-covered by S³; odd dimensions come naturally from frames L104, L156 Fits no tier: reason
  • X22S⁷ is the *join* of S³ with S³ (two frames plus a mixing parameter); the join of S¹ with S¹ is S³, so 1, 2, 4, 8 follows L157 Fits no tier: reason
  • X23The precise form of "N threads give 2N dimensions" is the product T₁ × … × Tₙ of thread surfaces; the 3D embedding picks out tuples where cursors coincide L185 Fits no tier: reason
  • X24The Tangle–Manifold–Graph triangle: manifold → tangle by surgery, graph → tangle by embedding, tangle → manifold by embedding or projection L114 Fits no tier: reason
    • X24.1predictions: crossing number bounds the manifold dimension needed; compression limits track crossing number; transfer works when tasks share tangle topology L116 Fits no tier: open question
  • X25Over/under at a crossing is the sign of a tetrahedron's determinant; a knot type is a pattern of orientation signs, an open region of configuration space L120-L121 Principium
  • X26Every knot has a minimal stick representation with integer coordinates: knot theory sits inside elementary lattice geometry L122 Occasio

V. Prior art and corrections

  • X27Up to dimension 4, theorems already encode manifolds as 3D threading plus labels with moves as coordinate changes (branched covers, Lickorish–Wallace, Kirby calculus, Piergallini, trisections); beyond 4 there is no comparable general theorem L126-L137 Fits no tier: precedent
  • X28ML prior art: Olah (untangling data), Naitzat et al. (topology layer by layer), Engels et al. (circle features), RASP and Tracr (programs compiled into weights) L141, L159 Fits no tier: precedent
  • X29Knotting needs codimension 2; 3D is privileged for fibres; every graph embeds in 3D without crossings L149 Fits no tier: reason
  • X30"The manifold" of a model is three objects (weight, activation, data); a knot encoding applies to the last two L151 Principium
  • X31Topology alone does not carry the program: a knotapel must be a knot *plus labels*, as Kirby diagrams carry integer framings L152 Principium
  • X32"It fits in 3D" is cheap; the claim needs a canonical embedding such as an equilibrium conformation L153, L355 Principium
  • X33Moderate realism: the form (diagram modulo moves) is real; weights and 3D tangles are instantiations, the tangle the most legible L162 Telos

VI. The mathematical frame

  • X34No 3D shape is faithfully isomorphic to a higher-dimensional one (Brouwer), so every 3D representation pays in one of four currencies: fine-scale detail, projection, configuration space, discrete presentation L166-L181 Principium
  • X35By Sard, a generator covering a higher-dimensional shape from fewer parameters must be non-smooth: the 3D-to-nD relation is "somehow fractal" by force L174 Principium
  • X36The real claim is about *which* generator: compression, locality, naturalness L183 for X9Principium
  • X37Only intrinsic geometry of the output matters (heads travel on it); no ambient n-space need exist; isometric outputs define program equivalence L187-L191 Load-bearing decision
  • X38Latent structure: the knotapel-to-output map is many-to-one; test by applying the same edit to two knotapels with identical outputs (act and potency) L193 Fits no tier: reason
  • X39A higher-dimensional manifold is an *ens rationis cum fundamento in re*, grounded in states, relations and refinements of real 3D bodies L195 Telos

VII. Plexity

  • X40Strongest candidate: plexity measures how fast geometric detail grows under refinement; plexity 1 = zooming reveals nothing new L199 Fits no tier: open question
  • X41Refinement exponent: N(ε) segments to stay within ε grows like ε^−e, with e = 0 (line), ½ (smooth), D (fractal); one mapping is plexity = 1/(1 + e) L201-L209 Fits no tier: reason
  • X42Keep *detail* (steps for an accuracy) apart from *generator size* (rule length); the bet is a compact generator yielding unlimited detail on demand L211 Principium
  • X43Other candidate measures: wavelet decay, superposition/interference, precision dependence, topology-versus-geometry share, rule tension L215-L219 Fits no tier: open question
  • X44One head on one thread can only trace a curve; multiple heads make a higher-dimensional output possible L221, L246 Fits no tier: reason

Since then: Claude's commentary, not in the source
  • X53–X57 are already paid for. The knotapel read-through (docs/knotapel.html, notes 58 and 59) found demos 110–112 failing in exactly these ways: over/under decided in one projection with silently overflowing 64-bit rationals (X53; 6₃'s spectrum was 37 polynomials in 64-bit, 6 in 128-bit), simplification that passed strands through each other (X55; 10 of 12 base polygons singular), and the mirror test as the checksum that exposed it (X57).
  • X65.3's support is false as stated. "No 3D shape has a finite symmetry group above 120" fails for prisms (order 4n, unbounded). True version: finite subgroups of O(3) are cyclic, dihedral or polyhedral. The prediction survives on better grounds: the 5-cube's group contains S₅, which does not embed in O(3) (A₅ does; the full icosahedral group is A₅ × Z/2, not S₅).
  • L190: a cube's edges are intrinsically flat. All intrinsic curvature sits at the 8 vertices (angle defect π/2 each, 4π total by Gauss–Bonnet); edges are extrinsic only. Under X37's intrinsic view this bears on X65.2: only vertices are intrinsic events.
  • X35 is too strong. A covering generator cannot be Lipschitz; the best regularity for a curve filling n dimensions is Hölder 1/n, which the Hilbert curve attains. So Hilbert is already optimal in that sense, and the efficiency comparison (X9) must measure something Hilbert is not optimal at: generator size per meaningful structure, edit locality, symmetry.
  • X44 needs its qualifier. One head traces a curve only for smooth or Lipschitz readouts; with refinement a single head can fill a cube (Peano). L247 says as much; L221's "matches a theorem" should name which readouts.
  • X41 conflates scheme and shape. ½ for smooth curves is the approximation order of straight segments (error ~h²), so every smooth curve gives ½ and arcs or splines change it. Recommendation for X69: three separate pre-registered measures — intrinsic roughness (box-counting dimension of the output), generator size (description length), and detail yield per refinement level relative to generator size. The bet (X42) is that the third is large while the second is small.
  • Precision for X27. Piergallini (1995): 4-fold covers branched over immersed surfaces; Iori–Piergallini (2002): 5-fold covers over embedded surfaces.
  • Rung 1 (X63) under the demo protocol: a committed predictions file first, naming which plexity measures should move as p rises and in which direction; X65.4's "either answer is informative" needs a stated expectation to count as a prediction.
  • Library needs map onto the house plan (see the companion sheet): magnus (built), fractio, dyadic rationals, quadratic fields, polynomials, exact linear algebra, Z/2 linear algebra for persistent homology, exact predicates, a knot library with PD codes and triangle moves.
