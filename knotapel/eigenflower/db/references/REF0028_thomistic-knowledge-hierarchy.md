## Description

The Thomistic distinction between three levels of knowledge provides the philosophical foundation for Eigenflower's "wisdom circuits" concept:

- **Scientia** (knowledge of particulars) -- "Socrates is mortal." Facts about specific entities.
- **Intellectus** (understanding of principles) -- "All men are mortal." General rules.
- **Sapientia** (wisdom -- knowledge of highest causes) -- the abstract structural patterns that organize all other knowledge. Not facts about any particular domain but principles by which everything is organized.

Abstract relational patterns like "asymmetric dependency," "part-whole composition," "cyclic return," "hierarchy of participation" appear across theology, philosophy, biology, physics, language, and social structure.

## Relevance to Eigenflower

- Wisdom circuits = sapientia-level patterns implemented as transparent, reusable ANN components
- A system with these patterns as explicit circuits has something like wisdom in the Thomistic sense
- The hierarchy explains WHY LLMs generalize: once internal circuits match abstract relational patterns, inputs from different domains share those circuits
- The generalization mechanism: domain-specific inputs route through domain-independent wisdom circuits
- The build order follows the hierarchy: start with deductive (most formal), then analogical, then fuzzy
