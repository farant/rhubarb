## Description

The circuit sharing thesis explains WHY LLMs exhibit emergent generalization:

1. There exist abstract relational patterns in reality (asymmetric dependency, part-whole, proportionality, etc.)
2. Once a model develops internal circuits matching these patterns, domain-specific inputs from different areas begin sharing them
3. More training data provides more instances of the same abstract patterns, strengthening shared circuits
4. At critical mass, circuits "click" and work for all instances of a pattern, including unseen ones -- that's emergence

Wisdom circuits are:
- **Domain-independent**: same circuit handles asymmetric dependency across family trees, causal chains, syllogistic inference
- **Reusable**: many domain-specific inputs route through the same circuit
- **Hierarchical**: some patterns are more abstract than others
- **The source of generalization**: new domains generalize immediately if their structure maps onto existing wisdom circuits

In Eigenflower, wisdom circuits could live in the syllogism layer (compiled tensor equations = reusable abstract logic), the fluency layer (learned language patterns), the interface (structural correspondences between linguistic form and logical content), and spanning both layers (analogy as proportionality relation requiring both fluency and logic).

## Relevance to Eigenflower

- This is the core theoretical insight behind the hybrid architecture
- The syllogism layer's compiled operators ARE wisdom circuits (Barbara is domain-independent)
- The GPTQ-as-CVP framework on cyclotomic lattices is the tool for decompiling trained models to find wisdom circuits
- The long game: wisdom circuits understood, compiled, and composable rather than discovered by gradient descent and trapped in opaque weights
