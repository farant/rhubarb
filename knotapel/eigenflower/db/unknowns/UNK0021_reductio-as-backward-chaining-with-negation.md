The transparency document describes reductio ad absurdum as "backward chaining through the syllogism graph with negation." This is a simplification. Reductio requires: (1) assuming the negation of what you want to prove, (2) deriving a contradiction from that assumption plus known premises, (3) concluding the original proposition by discharging the assumption. Step 3 involves a meta-logical operation (assumption discharge) that doesn't naturally fit into a tensor equation framework where all operations are forward computations on truth values. Can the syllogism graph represent hypothetical reasoning (assume P, derive contradiction, conclude not-P)? Or does reductio need a separate mechanism from the standard forward/backward chaining?
