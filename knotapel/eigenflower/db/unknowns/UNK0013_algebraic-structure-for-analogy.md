The text says analogical reasoning has "known mathematical structure (morphisms between relations, proportionality)" and asks "can they be compiled transparently? What algebraic structure do they need?" But this is stated as an open question without even a candidate answer. Syllogistic reasoning maps cleanly to tensor equations because the logic is well-defined (valid forms, truth conditions). Analogical reasoning is fuzzier: A:B::C:D assertions can be more or less apt, and the criteria for "good analogy" are debated in philosophy. It is unknown whether there exists an algebraic formalism for analogy that is: (a) precise enough to compile, (b) expressive enough to capture Aquinas's analogical arguments, and (c) compatible with the exact arithmetic substrate.
