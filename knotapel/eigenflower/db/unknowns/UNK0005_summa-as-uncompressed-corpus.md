The Inverse Knowledge Search paper's key insight is described as: the Summa is already an "uncompressed reasoning corpus" — it doesn't hide its derivations like most text does. This claim is plausible (the Summa's structure is explicitly argumentative) but not verified. How much of the Summa's reasoning is explicit enough for the "uncompressed" label to hold? The sed contra / respondeo structure is explicit at the article level, but within each argument, steps may be implicit ("it is clear that...", "as was shown above..."). If the reasoning is only partially uncompressed, the 1/8 data efficiency result from the Early Experience paper may not transfer directly, since that result may depend on the training data being fully explicit in its reasoning.
