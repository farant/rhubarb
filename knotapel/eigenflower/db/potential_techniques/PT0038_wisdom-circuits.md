Abstract relational patterns ("wisdom circuits") that are domain-independent, reusable, hierarchical, and the source of generalization. "Parent is to child as cause is to effect as premise is to conclusion" -- all instances of "asymmetric dependency." A model with an internal circuit for asymmetric dependency can apply it to family trees, causal reasoning, and logical inference simultaneously.

Wisdom circuits exist at three levels:
1. In the syllogism layer: tensor logic equations ARE wisdom circuits (Barbara is domain-independent)
2. In the fluency layer: learned linguistic patterns (Latin ablative absolute = "simultaneous background condition")
3. In the interface: structural correspondences between linguistic form and logical content ("Omnis X est Y" -> universal affirmative)

The most interesting case: patterns spanning BOTH layers. Analogy "Grace is to the soul as light is to the eye" requires fluency (parsing the four terms) + syllogism layer (proportionality relation A:B::C:D).

In Thomistic terms: scientia (knowledge of particulars) -> intellectus (understanding of principles) -> sapientia (wisdom, knowledge of highest causes). Wisdom circuits are closer to sapientia -- structural principles that organize facts across domains.

The research program: start with deductive wisdom circuits (transparent by construction), then study analogical circuits, then decompile trained models to find existing wisdom circuits, then build them deliberately.
