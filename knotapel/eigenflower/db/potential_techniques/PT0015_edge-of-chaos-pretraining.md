Pretrain a model on data at intermediate complexity (Wolfram Class IV, "edge of chaos") to develop rich general-purpose internal representations. Models trained on complex data develop better representations than those trained on simple or maximally chaotic data (r=0.73 correlation with downstream reasoning performance).

Key finding: intelligence emerges from PREDICTING complexity, not from intelligent data. Training data generated by a deterministic 8-bit cellular automaton rule (no inherent intelligence) produces representations that transfer to reasoning and strategic tasks.

A 67k-parameter "Tiny" model (1 layer, 1 head, 64-dim embeddings) shows the same effect (r=0.71). Even tiny models benefit from complex training data.

For eigenflower: the Bible and Summa are extremely complex texts (multilingual, philosophical, self-referential, theological). Data COMPLEXITY matters more than quantity -- this validates the small corpus approach. Complexity measures (Lempel-Ziv, compression, Lyapunov, Krylov) could SCORE and CURATE passages to train on the most information-rich sections.

Models learn complex solutions even when simple ones exist, and this surplus complexity is what makes them useful for downstream tasks. 1-step prediction is sufficient -- task difficulty doesn't need to be high, DATA complexity is what matters.
