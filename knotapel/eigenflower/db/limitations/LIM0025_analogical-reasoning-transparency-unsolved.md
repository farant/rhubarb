The transparency requirement (LIM0011) demands DKC-level understanding of every component. Syllogistic deduction achieves this through tensor logic — every step is an exact algebraic operation. But analogical reasoning (A:B::C:D, proportionality) does not yet have a transparent architecture.

From 03-transparency-and-wisdom-circuits.md: "Syllogistic deduction is the easy case where mathematical clarity already exists. Analogical reasoning is harder but not necessarily opaque if you build the architecture right."

Analogy is one of Aquinas's most important reasoning modes. "Grace is to the soul as light is to the eye" is not a syllogism — it's a structural mapping between domains. It has its own logic (can't conclude identity from analogy, only similarity of relation).

The wisdom circuits framework proposes that analogical reasoning could have its own transparent component, but the mathematical structure is less clear:
- Syllogism = tensor equation (known, exact)
- Analogy = morphism between relations (mathematically definable, but how to compile it?)
- What algebraic structure supports analogical reasoning the way Z[zeta_8] supports Boolean computation?

Implications:
- Phase 1 (syllogistic reasoning) sidesteps this limitation entirely.
- Phases 2-3 will need to address analogy at least partially.
- The "decompile existing models" goal is partly motivated by this: find out how trained LLMs implement analogical reasoning, then build that mechanism deliberately.
- Category theory (morphisms between objects in different categories) is a natural mathematical framework for analogy, but compiling category-theoretic structure to ANN weights is an open research question.
