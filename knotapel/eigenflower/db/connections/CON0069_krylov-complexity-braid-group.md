D106 falsifies topological entropy (Lyapunov analog) as predictor of DKC computation. But Krylov complexity -- tracking OPERATOR growth rather than state-space divergence -- is structurally closer to what DKC measures.

BFS catalog growth (~(n-1)x per round, confirmed as braid group invariant across D99-D103) IS a Krylov-like growth rate for the operator algebra generated by braid generators. It measures how many independent operators (bracket values) the system needs to describe its evolution, not how far states diverge.

Lempel-Ziv complexity of braid WORDS (as strings over {sigma_1, sigma_1^{-1}, sigma_2, sigma_2^{-1}}) is also worth testing: D106 showed topological entropy of braids as dynamical systems is orthogonal. Would string complexity of braid words as symbol sequences correlate with computational participation?

This bridges Paper 3's complexity measures to DKC's algebraic framework. Krylov complexity would test whether operator growth rate predicts reservoir quality where dynamical divergence rate does not.
