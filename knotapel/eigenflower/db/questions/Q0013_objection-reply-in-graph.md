The Summa's articles are structured as: objections (videtur quod), counter-authority (sed contra), main argument (respondeo), and replies to objections (ad primum/secundum/tertium). The objections are deliberately WRONG syllogisms — their premises or logical form are shown to be flawed in the replies. Does the graph store these as "refuted syllogisms" with error annotations? Does it store the error type (equivocation, false premise, invalid form)? Or does it only store the correct conclusions from the respondeo? The objection-reply structure is pedagogically and argumentatively essential to the Summa — omitting it loses a huge amount of information. Including it requires the graph to represent not just valid reasoning but also catalogued errors and their corrections.
