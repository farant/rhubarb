related: []
---

If only 30% of the Summa's reasoning is formal syllogisms, the syllogism graph is sparse and the fluency layer does heavy lifting for the other 70%. If 80% is syllogistic, the graph is rich and the fluency layer is mostly a translator. This fraction determines the architecture's balance of work. An empirical answer requires sampling articles from the Summa and classifying each argument step as: syllogistic deduction, analogy, argument from authority, reductio ad absurdum, distinguo, or other. Even a sample of 50-100 articles would give a useful distribution. Aquinas himself taxonomizes these modes, so the classification criteria exist.
