## VI. The mathematical frame

- [ ] X34 No 3D shape is faithfully isomorphic to a higher-dimensional one (Brouwer), so every 3D representation pays in one of four currencies: fine-scale detail, projection, configuration space, discrete presentation (L166-L181)  {PRINCIPIUM}
- [ ] X35 By Sard, a generator covering a higher-dimensional shape from fewer parameters must be non-smooth: the 3D-to-nD relation is "somehow fractal" by force (L174)  {PRINCIPIUM}
- [ ] X36 The real claim is about *which* generator: compression, locality, naturalness (L183)  {PRINCIPIUM > X9}
- [ ] X37 Only intrinsic geometry of the output matters (heads travel on it); no ambient n-space need exist; isometric outputs define program equivalence (L187-L191)  {DECRETUM}
- [ ] X38 Latent structure: the knotapel-to-output map is many-to-one; test by applying the same edit to two knotapels with identical outputs (act and potency) (L193)  {nulla:ratio}
- [ ] X39 A higher-dimensional manifold is an *ens rationis cum fundamento in re*, grounded in states, relations and refinements of real 3D bodies (L195)  {TELOS}

## VII. Plexity

- [ ] X40 Strongest candidate: plexity measures how fast geometric detail grows under refinement; plexity 1 = zooming reveals nothing new (L199)  {nulla:quaestio}
- [ ] X41 Refinement exponent: N(ε) segments to stay within ε grows like ε^−e, with e = 0 (line), ½ (smooth), D (fractal); one mapping is plexity = 1/(1 + e) (L201-L209)  {nulla:ratio}
- [ ] X42 Keep *detail* (steps for an accuracy) apart from *generator size* (rule length); the bet is a compact generator yielding unlimited detail on demand (L211)  {PRINCIPIUM}
- [ ] X43 Other candidate measures: wavelet decay, superposition/interference, precision dependence, topology-versus-geometry share, rule tension (L215-L219)  {nulla:quaestio}
- [ ] X44 One head on one thread can only trace a curve; multiple heads make a higher-dimensional output possible (L221, L246)  {nulla:ratio}

## XII. Since then (my commentary, not in the source)

Review given in the session of 2026-10-05, recorded here so it survives
the context window; none of it edits the source.

- **X53–X57 are already paid for.** The knotapel read-through
  (`docs/knotapel.html`, notes 58 and 59) found demos 110–112 failing in
  exactly these ways: over/under decided in one projection with silently
  overflowing 64-bit rationals (X53; 6₃'s spectrum was 37 polynomials in
  64-bit, 6 in 128-bit), simplification that passed strands through each
  other (X55; 10 of 12 base polygons singular), and the mirror test as the
  checksum that exposed it (X57).
- **X65.3's support is false as stated.** "No 3D shape has a finite
  symmetry group above 120" fails for prisms (order 4n, unbounded). True
  version: finite subgroups of O(3) are cyclic, dihedral or polyhedral.
  The prediction survives on better grounds: the 5-cube's group contains
  S₅, which does not embed in O(3) (A₅ does; the full icosahedral group is
  A₅ × Z/2, not S₅).
- **L190: a cube's edges are intrinsically flat.** All intrinsic curvature
  sits at the 8 vertices (angle defect π/2 each, 4π total by Gauss–Bonnet);
  edges are extrinsic only. Under X37's intrinsic view this bears on
  X65.2: only vertices are intrinsic events.
- **X35 is too strong.** A covering generator cannot be Lipschitz; the best
  regularity for a curve filling n dimensions is Hölder 1/n, which the
  Hilbert curve attains. So Hilbert is already optimal in that sense, and
  the efficiency comparison (X9) must measure something Hilbert is not
  optimal at: generator size per meaningful structure, edit locality,
  symmetry.
- **X44 needs its qualifier.** One head traces a curve only for smooth or
  Lipschitz readouts; with refinement a single head can fill a cube
  (Peano). L247 says as much; L221's "matches a theorem" should name which
  readouts.
- **X41 conflates scheme and shape.** ½ for smooth curves is the
  approximation order of straight segments (error ~h²), so every smooth
  curve gives ½ and arcs or splines change it. Recommendation for X69:
  three separate pre-registered measures — intrinsic roughness (box-counting
  dimension of the output), generator size (description length), and
  detail yield per refinement level relative to generator size. The bet
  (X42) is that the third is large while the second is small.
- **Precision for X27.** Piergallini (1995): 4-fold covers branched over
  immersed surfaces; Iori–Piergallini (2002): 5-fold covers over embedded
  surfaces.
- **Rung 1 (X63) under the demo protocol:** a committed predictions file
  first, naming which plexity measures should move as p rises and in which
  direction; X65.4's "either answer is informative" needs a stated
  expectation to count as a prediction.
- Library needs map onto the house plan (see the companion sheet): magnus
  (built), fractio, dyadic rationals, quadratic fields, polynomials, exact
  linear algebra, Z/2 linear algebra for persistent homology, exact
  predicates, a knot library with PD codes and triangle moves.
