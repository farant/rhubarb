## Where we ended up

The working thesis: higher-dimensional shapes are best explained as generated by 3D threads, and a "knotapel" is a program whose execution yields those shapes. The project is now framed as engineering a representation that fits well, not proving that one exists.

- **What is already settled.** Any reasonable compact shape can be generated from threads (Hahn–Mazurkiewicz), and every compact smooth manifold is the configuration space of some simple mechanism (Kapovich–Millson). Universality is free.
- **What is open.** Whether knotapels are an *efficient* representation: compact, local, natural, and better than baselines such as Hilbert curves, Fourier or wavelet encodings.
- **The object.** A knotapel is a *framed geometric knot*: a specific conformation of strands whose geometry, not just topology, carries information.
- **The interpreter.** A pure function: (knotapel, positions of k heads, optional refinement level) → n coordinates. One head yields only a curve; k heads can yield a k-dimensional shape.
- **The pipeline.** Hypergraph source → textile-style physical relaxation (the compiler) → geometric knot (the executable) → interpreter → intrinsic n-manifold (the output).
- **Plexity.** Best candidate: how fast detail grows under refinement. Straight lines yield nothing on zoom (plexity 1); smooth curves and fractals yield progressively more.
- **First experiments.** Generate a 5-ball and a 5-cube, then the family between them, and compare generator complexity against a Hilbert-curve baseline.

## Origin and vocabulary

The vocabulary was coined in [Hyperdimensional Physics and AI Manifolds](https://claude.ai/chat/25cd5bc8-88f2-41ec-9a00-aeb1b6452efc) (July 2025). Its starting claim: knots exist only in 3D, so higher-dimensional structures may be better read as knotted, braided 3D fiber objects, with the "extra dimensions" recording adjacency, crossings and trajectories of strands.

| Term | Meaning as coined |
| --- | --- |
| Knotapel | The 3D fiber structure encoding a model's weights (from Old English *cnotta*, knot) |
| Threadshapes | Patterns that attention heads "embroider" through the knotapel during a forward pass; they can form knots not present in the weights |
| Plexity | A measure of how information flows through a knotapel; plexity 1 = single-threaded, program-like flow |
| Prift | Prime + weft: the primitive building blocks a knotapel is composed from |
| Gweith | The sub-region of a knotapel being actively designed |
| Embroidery wells | Regions that pull threadshapes from different domains together (cross-domain generalization) |
| Topology deduplication wells | Regions where similar structures merge during training |
| Portal prifts | Structures linking distant regions, which look like "teleporting between layers" |
| Threadshape compilers | Embedding layers that turn tokens into threadshapes and back |

Other claims from that conversation:

- Weights are a "hyperdimensional shadow" of the 3D knotapel, which is why one knot can be spread across many dimensions and why interpretability is hard.
- A knotapel ignores what inputs mean, so 11111→22222 and aaaaa→bbbbb share one shape. This was called a useful lossiness.
- Plexity zoom: at plexity 1, zooming in reveals nothing new; below 1, each zoom level is only partly resolved, and information yield per zoom is inversely related to plexity.
- Plexity was later proposed as complex-valued: a real (sequential) part plus an imaginary (associative) part.
- Language as DNA, training as RNA: language carries a semantic knotapel that training transcribes into weights.
- The goal: compute weights directly from intent instead of finding them by training, starting from the analytically solvable plexity-1 case.

## Thread coordinates and the triangle

The July conversation never worked out an actual coordinate scheme; four later conversations did.

**1. Thread coordinates** ([Milnor's Exotic 7-Dimensional Spheres](https://claude.ai/chat/0775aa52-b718-4a16-8896-abee2e3ec870), July 2025).

- Each thread is a tube; a point on it is (z, θ): position along it, angle around it.
- Two threads give (z₁, θ₁, z₂, θ₂), so 4D; N threads give 2N dimensions.
- Crossings couple the coordinates: at a crossing the two systems are locked, and between crossings their relationship varies as a gradient. "A higher-dimensional manifold is dimensions tangled together; the crossing pattern is the topology."
- Proposed source of odd dimensions: where a thread folds back on itself, one 3D point has two thread positions.
- Torsion and pulling were proposed to take a two-thread system from 4D to 5D, leading to crochet as the model.

**2. Frames and spheres** ([Work in progress](https://claude.ai/chat/5fb32aee-8547-435f-96b0-9ed6858a307f), February 2026).

- Each strand point carries its position plus the orientation of its local frame, plus its relationship to other strands.
- Position and frame give 3 parameters; the frame alone is SO(3), double-covered by S³ (unit quaternions).
- Two strands were linked to S⁷ via the Hopf fibration, reading 1, 2, 4, 8 as crossing complexity rather than spatial dimension. A formula n = 2s + w (dimension, strand count, writhe) appears in this thread.

**3. Applied to LLMs** ([1-Lipschitz Neural Networks](https://claude.ai/chat/48a92529-88f7-45e2-bd75-9af4e1db8c56), July 2025).

- A 4096-dimensional embedding split as 64 threads × 3 position dimensions, about 1000 pairwise crossings × 3 features, plus higher-order invariants.
- Attention as deciding which threads braid (query/key) and how they cross (value); depth as more chances to cross.

**4. The Tangle–Manifold–Graph triangle** ([rhubarb chat](https://claude.ai/chat/70d4a9fd-0349-45ab-8049-dbb46f557660), February 2026).

- Manifold → tangle via surgery (Lickorish–Wallace); graph → tangle via 3D embedding; tangle → manifold via embedding or projection.
- "Thread Realism": only the tangle vertex is real; manifolds and graphs are descriptions.
- Predictions: crossing number bounds the manifold dimension needed; compression limits track crossing number; transfer learning works when tasks share tangle topology.

**5. Stick knots and constructive geometry** ([Understanding category theory](https://claude.ai/chat/6b489b3f-4548-4324-a41e-d29d52870906), March 2026).

- Over/under at each crossing = the sign of a tetrahedron's determinant, so a knot type is a pattern of orientation signs.
- Knot type is fixed by *inequalities*: each type occupies an open region of configuration space, bounded by walls where a crossing changes.
- Every knot has a minimal stick representation with integer coordinates, so knot theory sits inside elementary lattice geometry.

## Prior art: manifolds drawn as knots

Up to dimension 4, established theorems already encode manifolds as 3D threading plus discrete labels, with a set of moves acting as coordinate changes. Beyond dimension 4 there is no comparable general theorem.

| Result | What it says | Dimension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alexander (1920); Hilden, Montesinos (1974) | Every closed orientable 3-manifold is a 3-fold branched cover of S³ over a knot or link | 3 |
| Lickorish–Wallace | Every closed orientable 3-manifold comes from surgery on a link in S³ | 3 |
| Kirby calculus (1978) | A 4-manifold is described by a framed link in 3D (loops labelled with integers); two diagrams give the same manifold exactly when related by Kirby moves | 4 |
| Piergallini (1995); Iori–Piergallini (2002) | Every closed oriented 4-manifold is a branched cover of S⁴ over a knotted surface, watched as a movie of 3D tangles | 4 |
| Gay–Kirby trisections (2012) | 4-manifolds from three families of curves on a surface | 4 |
| Brieskorn (1966) | The trefoil is the link of z² + w³ = 0; the same construction one level up yields Milnor's 28 exotic 7-spheres | 7 |
| Kapovich–Millson (2002) | Every compact smooth manifold, any dimension, is a component of the configuration space of a planar linkage | any |
| Hahn–Mazurkiewicz (1914) | Every compact, connected, locally connected space is a continuous image of an interval (one thread) | any |

Examples of Kirby diagrams: the 4-sphere is the empty diagram; CP² is an unknot labelled +1; S² × S² is the Hopf link with both loops labelled 0. Exotic structures and Akbulut corks were discovered by moving these knotted circles around. Standard text: Gompf & Stipsicz, *4-Manifolds and Kirby Calculus* (1999).

In machine learning, the closest prior work is Chris Olah's essay "Neural Networks, Manifolds, and Topology" (2014), where a network must untangle linked or knotted data; Naitzat, Zhitnikov & Lim, "Topology of Deep Neural Networks" (2020), which measures topology layer by layer; Engels et al., "Not All Language Model Features Are Linear" (2024), which found circle-shaped features for days and months; and RASP (2021) with DeepMind's Tracr (2023), which compile programs directly into transformer weights.

## Corrections to the original framework

Several earlier claims needed narrowing or reversing; none of the corrections undermines the core idea.

| Original claim | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Knots only exist in 3D | Knotting needs codimension 2: 1D strands knot in 3D, 2D surfaces knot in 4D. 3D is privileged for fibers specifically. The stronger supporting fact: every graph embeds in 3D without crossings. |
| Weights are a "shadow" of the knotapel | A shadow loses dimensions; weights have more. Better: weights are a coordinate description, and many weight settings describe the same function (neuron permutation, rescaling). |
| "The manifold" of a model | Three different objects: weight space (a model is one point in it), activation space, and the data manifold. A knot encoding applies to the last two, not to a single point. |
| Topology alone carries the program | Networks depend on magnitudes. A knotapel must be a knot *plus labels*, as Kirby diagrams carry integer framings. |
| "It fits in 3D" | Cheap: anything finite fits. The claim needs a canonical embedding, such as an equilibrium conformation. |
| Plexity 1 is the single-threaded ceiling | Plexity drifted between determinism, threading and precision. Single- vs multi-threaded is scheduling, not computational power. Needs one definition (see Plexity). |
| Complex-valued plexity | Proposed but never given meaning; e.g. why two associative regions would multiply to negative plexity. |
| A fold creates an odd dimension | A fold is a self-coincidence, not a new axis; a thread paired with itself is 4D whether or not it folds. Odd dimensions come naturally from frames (position + frame = 3). |
| S⁷ is a submanifold of S³ × S³ | S⁷ is the *join* of S³ with S³: two 3D frames plus one mixing parameter t ∈ \[0,1\]. The join of S¹ with S¹ is S³, so the sequence 1, 2, 4, 8 follows. |
| 4096 = threads + crossings + invariants | The split was chosen to sum to 4096; illustrative, not derived. |
| No one treats weights as a programming language | RASP and Tracr compile programs into weights; Neural Turing Machines and Universal Transformers address memory and variable depth. |
| Language "evolved" as compression | The framework does not need that claim. The Feb 2026 framing is cleaner: grammar is a "dehydrated" network, rehydrated in a brain or silicon. |

On the metaphysical claim "only the tangle is real": a 3D tangle is itself one chosen picture among many isotopic ones. What they share is the form, the diagram modulo moves. A moderate-realist reading: the form is real, and weights and 3D tangles are instantiations, the tangle being the most legible one.

## The mathematical frame

No 3D shape can be faithfully *isomorphic* to a higher-dimensional one, so every 3D representation must pay for the extra dimensions in one of four currencies.

**Brouwer's invariance of dimension (1911–12).** No continuous, reversible map with continuous inverse exists between spaces of different dimension. The history shows the boundary:

- Cantor (1877): a line and a square have the same number of points (interleave digits), but the pairing tears space apart.
- Peano (1890): a continuous curve can fill a square, but it hits some points more than once.
- Brouwer: continuity plus reversibility together are impossible across dimensions.

**Sard's theorem.** A smooth map from a lower-dimensional space covers measure zero. So any generator that covers a higher-dimensional shape from fewer parameters must be non-smooth at every scale: fractal, like Peano and Hilbert curves. The intuition that the 3D-to-nD relation is "somehow fractal" is forced, not optional.

| Route | What is given up | Where it appears in the framework |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fine-scale detail (Cantor) | Continuity; extra dimensions live at ever-finer scales | Plexity and zoom |
| Projection (Peano) | Reversibility; many-to-one | The "shadow" framing |
| Configuration space | Meaning: points become *states* of the object, not points in it | Thread-cursor coordinates; Kapovich–Millson |
| Discrete presentation | Continuity of the map; a drawing plus labels rebuilds the shape | Kirby diagrams |

**Generators.** Hahn–Mazurkiewicz makes the bare claim "every n-dimensional shape is derivable from a 3D generator" true but cheap. The real claim is about *which* generator: compression (small generator), locality (nearby points from nearby parts), naturalness (structure that corresponds to real features). Refinement by repeated invocation matches how Hilbert curves are built, and computable analysis already represents points as procedures yielding ever-finer approximations.

**Configuration spaces.** The precise version of "N threads give 2N dimensions": a point of the manifold is a simultaneous position on every thread, i.e. the product T₁ × … × Tₙ of thread surfaces. The 3D embedding picks out the tuples where cursors coincide; crossings and coupling live there.

**The intrinsic view.** The output manifold is something heads travel on, so only its intrinsic geometry matters: connectivity plus metric, as in Gauss's Theorema Egregium and Riemannian geometry. Consequences:

- No ambient n-dimensional space ever has to exist; it stays bookkeeping, as the thesis holds.
- Cube and sphere remain distinguishable: on a cube's surface curvature concentrates at corners and edges; on a sphere it is spread evenly.
- Purely extrinsic differences (a flat sheet vs the same sheet rolled) are invisible; two knotapels with isometric outputs compute the same thing, which defines program equivalence.

**Latent structure.** The knotapel-to-output map is many-to-one, so a knotapel can carry structure the output never shows. A knotted tube and a plain tube are intrinsically identical, yet the knotted one cannot be developed into certain shapes without cutting. Functionally identical networks likewise respond differently to further training. Test: apply the same edit to two knotapels with identical outputs; if the outputs now differ, the difference was meaningful latent structure, otherwise gauge. Scholastically, the output is the knotapel's act and the latent structure its potency.

**Ontology.** "Higher dimensions are bookkeeping" is consistent with all of the above. A higher-dimensional manifold is then an *ens rationis cum fundamento in re*: a real intelligible structure grounded in the states, relations and refinements of real 3D bodies.

## Plexity, refined

The strongest candidate definition: plexity measures how fast geometric detail grows under refinement, with plexity 1 meaning zooming in reveals nothing new.

**The refinement exponent.** Let N(ε) be the number of straight segments needed to stay within distance ε of a curve. As ε shrinks, N grows like ε to a negative power:

| Curve | Growth of N(ε) | Exponent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight line | Stays at 1 | 0 |
| Smooth curve | Like 1/√ε (a chord of length h deviates by about κh²/8) | ½ |
| Fractal of dimension D | Like ε⁻ᴰ | D ≥ 1 |

One simple mapping is plexity = 1/(1 + exponent); the exact formula should be settled with data. For smooth curves the segment count is roughly proportional to the integral of √κ along the curve, so the refinement cost itself carries information about how the curve bends.

**Approximation versus information.** Two quantities must be kept apart: *detail* (steps needed for a given accuracy) and *generator size* (length of the rule producing them). A circle needs ever more segments but carries 3 numbers; a Koch curve shows new structure at every scale from a tiny rule; a Brownian path has genuinely growing information. The knotapel bet is a compact generator yielding unlimited detail on demand.

**Other candidates, possibly as separate measures:**

- *Wavelet decay*: how fast detail coefficients fall as scale shrinks; a standard way to measure roughness and fractal dimension, computable on real activations.
- *Superposition / interference*: features ≤ dimensions gives clean channels (plexity 1); features > dimensions gives leaking. Grounded in interpretability research on superposition.
- *Precision dependence*: a discrete program gives the same answer at any number of decimal places; test by quantizing step by step and measuring the cost per removed bit.
- *Topology versus geometry share*: plexity 1 is all topology, perfectly crisp; lower plexity carries more information in continuous geometry.
- *Rule tension*: in a subdivision scheme, one tension parameter moves the limit curve from smooth to fractal, making plexity a property of the rule.

**One head, one curve.** The July claim that plexity 1 means a single head turns out to match a theorem: one head on one thread can only trace a one-dimensional path through the output. Multiple heads are what make a genuinely higher-dimensional output possible.

## Architecture

The language is a hypergraph that physics compiles into a geometric knot; a pure-function interpreter reads points of an intrinsic manifold off that knot.

&#91;embedded content: knotapel pipeline · source to output\]

Edits to the source change the compiled shape: weight edits should move it smoothly, structural edits can change crossings discretely. The closest real analogy is protein folding: a sequence compiled by physics into a 3D fold whose exact geometry does the work.

**Data model.** A program is a knotapel: a framed conformation built from prifts (tangles), carrying discrete labels. It has three layers:

1. *Topology*: crossings, linking, knot types. Discrete and robust, like built-in error correction.
2. *Intrinsic geometry*: curvature, torsion, framing, relative distances. The content that determines the output.
3. *Pose*: position and orientation. Never affects the output's shape; at most maps equivariantly onto its placement.

**Compiler.** Precedents are force-directed graph drawing (Fruchterman–Reingold), knot relaxation (KnotPlot) and discrete elastic rods. Requirements:

- Deterministic: fixed initial layout and integrator, or an energy landscape with one minimum per source.
- Hyperedges compile to n strands meeting at one contact region (a braid or junction).
- Occurrence weights need a physical meaning: tension, stiffness, thickness or rest length. This is the key design decision.
- Incremental: a changed relation re-relaxes only its gweith.

**Interpreter.** Signature: (knotapel, positions of k heads, optional refinement level) → ℝⁿ.

- One head on one thread can only trace a curve, however many coordinates it returns (Sard). Reading non-local data, such as distance to the nearest strand, makes the curve richer but keeps it a curve.
- k heads can reach k-dimensional shapes smoothly; refinement depth reaches further at the cost of fractal detail.
- Read coordinates from intrinsic quantities (arc length, curvature, torsion, framing, inter-strand distances) so the output is invariant under moving the knotapel. Curvature and torsion fix a curve up to rigid motion.
- Scale is an open choice: enlarging a knotapel divides its curvatures, so scale is either content or pose.
- Where equivariance is wanted, 3D rotations act on 5-dimensional space via the spin-2 representation (traceless symmetric 3×3 matrices). They supply only 3 of the 5-ball's 10 rotational freedoms.

The project is engineering against mathematical constraints, not a pass/fail proof. By the invariance theorem of Kolmogorov complexity, any two universal languages differ by at most a constant in description length; the claim of good fit lives entirely in that constant.

## Representation and exactness

Recommended data model: integer lattice control polygons, a deterministic subdivision rule whose tension is part of the program, a topology-authoritative crossing code, and exact integer arithmetic throughout.

**Vocabulary.**

- In knot theory a *knot* is the equivalence class; a specific instance is a *knot embedding* or *conformation*.
- *Tangle* has a technical meaning (Conway): a knot piece in a ball with ends on its surface. Tangles are the natural formal home for prifts.
- *Geometric* or *physical knot theory* studies exactly how the rope lies: ropelength and ideal knots, elastic (Kirchhoff) rods.
- *Framed knots* or *ribbons* track twist around the centerline, matching the "angle of the normal" coordinate.
- The working description: a knotapel is a **framed conformation built from tangles**.

**Geometry and topology are locked together by Lk = Tw + Wr** (Călugăreanu–White–Fuller). Linking number is topological; twist and writhe are geometric. Their sum is fixed, so deformation can trade twist for writhe but never change the total. This is the kind of law the framework wants, and it sits close to the n = 2s + w formula.

**Curve encodings.**

| Encoding | Strength | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Splines (Bézier, B-spline) | Standard; convex-hull property gives cheap intersection checks | Arc length usually not exact |
| Pythagorean-hodograph curves (Farouki) | Exact polynomial arc length; some spatial PH curves have exact frames; built from quaternion polynomials | More constrained shapes |
| Intrinsic (curvature, torsion vs arc length) | Pose-free by construction (curves are fixed up to rigid motion) | Rebuilding needs integration; use helix and clothoid pieces |
| Fourier knots | Every knot type has one; refinement = more harmonics | Global waves, not local detail |
| Discrete elastic rods (Bergou et al. 2008) | Physics already solved; fits the textile compiler | Simulation, not a closed form |
| Subdivision curves (Chaikin; Dyn–Levin–Gregory four-point) | Refinement is literally re-invocation; tension sets smooth vs fractal; dyadic rationals allow exact integer arithmetic | Must guard topology during refinement |

**Keeping over/under unambiguous.** The "which line is on top" failures come from projections, floating point, or edits that pass strands through each other.

1. Never decide over/under in a projection; use exact integer orientation (determinant-sign) tests, and change view direction when a projection is degenerate.
2. Store the crossing code (Gauss or PD code) as the source of truth; geometry must realize it, and edits that break it are rejected.
3. Edit only by triangle moves: replace edge AB with AC + CB only if triangle ABC meets no other part of the knot.
4. Lattice knots: unit-step self-avoiding polygons on the integer cubic lattice cannot have ambiguous crossings at all.
5. Recompute invariants (linking numbers, writhe, Alexander polynomial) after every operation as checksums.

**Number systems.**

- Sphere-and-plane constructions in 3D produce exactly the constructible numbers, the same field as plane compass-and-straightedge: square roots yes, cube roots no.
- Regular polygons with primes 2, 3, 5 only (Gauss–Wantzel): 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 16, 20 sides, close to the 5-smooth primitives.
- Icosahedral symmetry is exact in ℚ(√5); the binary icosahedral group's quaternions (icosians) form the E₈ lattice under a suitable norm.
- Fix one field (ℚ(√5), possibly with ℚ(√2) or the eighth-root-of-unity integers already in rhubarb) rather than open-ended nesting, whose cost doubles per root.
- Angles and arc lengths escape constructible fields (π); represent angles by the tangent half-angle t, with cos = (1 − t²)/(1 + t²) and sin = 2t/(1 + t²).

**Threads as a number system.** Rational tangles correspond exactly to the rationals (Conway: twist adds 1, rotate maps x to −1/x); knots factor uniquely into primes (Schubert 1949); braids are orderable (Dehornoy); quipu kept base-10 records in knotted cords. Knotapels differ in storing data in geometry as well: a protected topological layer plus a continuous geometric layer.

## Experiments

Start with curve representations and readout functions on a single thread, then add heads, constraints and refinement; every stage is measured against fixed baselines.

**Staged plan.**

1. **One head, one thread.** Try curve representations and readouts mapping a thread position to n coordinates. Output: curves in n-space. Tests curvature, torsion, framing and the plexity exponent.
2. **Two heads.** Surfaces: a torus from two closed loops, a square from two open strands, a sphere from a strand plus a loop with collapse at the ends.
3. **k heads plus crossing constraints.** Coupled coordinates, where the Milnor-chat crossing scheme matters.
4. **Refinement.** Add a depth parameter; test whether fewer heads plus refinement can match more heads.

**Closed loops versus open strands.** A head on a closed loop ranges over a circle; on an open strand, over an interval. Five independent heads on strands give exactly a 5-cube (corners where heads sit at endpoints); on loops, a 5-torus; a ball needs collapse. Cubes come from bounded translation, spheres from rotation.

**Target ladder.**

| Rung | Targets | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5-ball, 5-cube, and the Lᵖ family between them (p = 2 sphere, p → ∞ cube) | Pure geometry: topology is identical, only corners differ |
| 2 | Orthotopes and ellipsoids; 5D rotations of the cube | Stretching should change parameters, not structure; rotation tests coordinate dependence |
| 3 | Tori, Klein bottle | New topology; whether framing can encode non-orientability |
| 4 | Hopf fibration | A space already made of linked circles; a framework that cannot reproduce it is off |
| 5 | Poincaré homology sphere (dodecahedral space) | Surgery on a trefoil; links dodecahedral and E₈ threads |
| 6 | Mandelbox (box fold + sphere fold), quaternion Julia sets, Mandelbulb | Fractals: tests plexity, not manifold representation |
| 7 | Brieskorn exotic 7-spheres | Whether smooth structure itself can be encoded; expected hardest |

**Measurements, fixed in advance.**

- Coverage: at refinement level k, is every target point within ε, with ε shrinking as k grows?
- Shape match: Hausdorff distance between generated cloud and target.
- Corners: do sharp features align with discrete events in the generator?
- Efficiency: strands, crossings and parameters needed, against Hilbert-curve, Fourier and wavelet baselines.
- Invariance: stretching, rotation and pose changes should change only parameters.

**Predictions that can fail.**

- Stretching the target changes parameters, not structure.
- Sharp corners require discrete events (kinks, crossings, contacts); the cube's generator complexity tracks its 32 vertices, 80 edges, 80 squares, 40 cubes, 10 four-faces.
- Symmetry lives in the readout and labels, not the 3D geometry: the 5-cube's symmetry group has 3,840 elements, but no 3D shape has a finite symmetry group above 120 (icosahedral).
- In the Lᵖ family, generator events appear either gradually or at a threshold as p rises; either answer is informative.

**Machine-learning experiments.**

- *Linked rings*: train small networks on linked or knotted point clouds, vary width, track topology layer by layer with persistent homology. Prediction: a minimum width below which the data cannot be untangled, related to knot complexity.
- *Compiled versus trained*: a Tracr-compiled program versus a trained network of the same shape on the same task; compare interference, quantization sensitivity, and whether one prift analysis describes both.
- *Thread decomposition*: express hidden states as positions on a few one-dimensional curves; look for continuity, coupling and multiplicity, building on the circle features already found.

**Formal side.** Define prifts as morphisms in a labelled tangle category; the Temperley–Lieb and bracket work in rhubarb is already the right algebra.

## Open decisions

These choices are undecided and shape everything downstream:

1. **Which object is the knotapel for ML work:** the data manifold through layers, the circuit graph, or something else.
2. **Plexity's definition:** refinement exponent, wavelet decay, interference, precision dependence, or several separate measures.
3. **What occurrence weights mean physically** in the textile compiler: tension, stiffness, thickness or rest length.
4. **Scale:** part of a knotapel's content (changes output shape) or of its pose (only enlarges output).
5. **Readout functions:** which intrinsic quantities a head reads to produce coordinates.
6. **Curve encoding:** subdivision on integer lattice control polygons is the current recommendation, not yet tested.
7. **Canonical embedding:** whether equilibrium conformation is canonical enough for crossing-number claims.
